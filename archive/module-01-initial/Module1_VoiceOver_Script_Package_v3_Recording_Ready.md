# LESSON M1-L1 Why Statistics in Manufacturing

## M1L1-S01 (10 sec)
In manufacturing, no process produces exactly the same result every time. [PAUSE-1] These natural differences are called VARIATION.

## M1L1-S02 (15 sec)
Consider a shaft with a target diameter of fifty millimeters. [PAUSE-1] Even when the process is stable, measurements vary slightly around the target.

## M1L1-S03 (15 sec)
Variation can come from materials, machines, methods, measurement systems, operators, and environmental conditions.

## M1L1-S04 (20 sec)
Statistics helps engineers convert raw measurements into meaningful information. [PAUSE-1] This allows us to separate normal variation from abnormal conditions.

## M1L1-S05 Assessment Feedback
Correct. A stable process still contains natural variation. Stable does not mean identical.

# LESSON M1-L2 Types of Data

## M1L2-S01 (12 sec)
Manufacturing data generally fall into two categories: variable data and attribute data.

## M1L2-S02 (15 sec)
Variable data are measured on a continuous scale. Examples include length, diameter, temperature, pressure, and torque.

## M1L2-S03 (15 sec)
Attribute data are classified or counted. Examples include scratches, dents, cracks, and pass-fail inspection results.

## M1L2-S04 (15 sec)
Correct data classification is important because different data types require different statistical tools and control charts.

## M1L2-S05 Summary
Before selecting an SPC method, always determine whether the data are variable or attribute.

# LESSON M1-L3 Descriptive Statistics

## M1L3-S01
Descriptive statistics allow engineers to summarize large amounts of information using a few meaningful indicators.

## M1L3-S02
The MEAN represents the average value and is commonly used to evaluate process centering.

## M1L3-S03
The MEDIAN represents the middle observation when data are arranged from lowest to highest.

## M1L3-S04
The RANGE represents the difference between the maximum and minimum values.

## M1L3-S05
STANDARD DEVIATION measures variation around the average value.

## M1L3-S06
Two processes can have the same average but very different levels of variation.

## M1L3-S07
Lower variation generally improves consistency, quality performance, and customer satisfaction.

## M1L3-S08 Sigma Simulation
Move the slider and observe the histogram. As sigma increases, the distribution becomes wider and variation increases.

## M1L3-S09 Assessment Feedback
The correct answer is the process with the narrower distribution because it demonstrates less variation.

# LESSON M1-L4 Data Visualization

## M1L4-S01
Visualizing data helps engineers identify patterns that may be difficult to detect in tables of numbers.

## M1L4-S02 Histogram
A histogram displays how data are distributed across different measurement ranges.

## M1L4-S03 Pareto Chart
A Pareto chart helps prioritize improvement efforts by highlighting the most significant contributors to a problem.

## M1L4-S04 Scatter Plot
A scatter plot helps determine whether a relationship exists between two variables.

## M1L4-S05 Box Plot
A box plot summarizes variation, median performance, and potential outliers.
